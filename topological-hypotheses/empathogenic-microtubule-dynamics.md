##  ⚛️💖🌀 Empathogenic States and Microtubule Dynamics

### Observations
Beyond binding to synaptic sites, empathogens (e.g., phenethylamines) can bind to microtubules. Research indicates they are similar to the microtubule destabilizing agent colchine [1](#ref1). The controversial yet fascinating Orch-OR theory posits that microtubules can directly influence consciousness through quantum summation of objective orchestration in microtubules [2](#ref2).

Children seem to have a natively empathogenic, curiosity-driven personality that closely mirrors the effects of empathogenic compounds and tryptamines. The brain dominantly expresses MAP1B and specific juvenile isoforms of Tau in childhood. These proteins bind to microtubules more loosely, allowing for high dynamic instability.

### Hypothesis
It is hypothesized that empathogenic and psychedelic compounds modulate conscious and emotional states by directly altering the structural, electronic, and phononic dynamics of the microtubule lattice. The specific topography of the molecular substitutions dictates the vector of this quantum-chemical interaction. For instance, a methylenedioxy substitution provides a planar, electron-dense configuration that induces a horizontal perturbation of delocalized $\pi$-electrons within tubulin's hydrophobic pockets. In addition, a heavy halogen substitution (e.g., the bromine atom in 2-CB) introduces anisotropic sigma-hole dynamics; its large, polarizable electron cloud generates a complex, reverberating vertical-horizontal electrostatic perturbation.

Conversely, other compounds with electronegative substitutions (e.g., phenothiazines) are thought to disrupt microtubule dynamics and act as a dampener. Delocalized configurations that increase stability and binding affinity of aromatic compounds (e.g., methylenedioxy groups or halogenation of phenethylamines) seem to have the effect of bringing back juvenile isoforms of neurotransmitters, and perhaps microtubule topology as well.

Phenethylamines and tryptamines seem to uniquely bring objective consciousness back to a childlike state, lacking the rebound effects of traditional pharmaceuticals. It is hypothesized that these compounds may induce tubulin back into a native conformation, which aligns with the research showing that psychedelics reopen critical periods. This research also proposes that neurodivergent phenotypes may retain these early native microtubule configuration states, which may explain the subjective experiences of retaining childlike qualities (e.g., friendliess and a sense of wonder in WS, hyperfascination and neoteny in ASD).

### Future Ideas
We should examine differences in microtubule and cytoskeletal topology among individuals with ASD and WS, using neurotypical phenotypes as a control group. For instance, we could examine genetic and epigenetic factors unique to or overexpressed in neurodivergent phenotypes that relate to MAP1B and juvenile conformations of Tau in microtubules. Using tools such as Dr. Pellegrini's advanced epigenetic computational software, we can map the epigenetic changes that change microtubule dynamics from the childlike dynamic configuration (three microtubule-binding repeats (3R-Tau)) to the rigid adult framework (4-repeat-Tau).
