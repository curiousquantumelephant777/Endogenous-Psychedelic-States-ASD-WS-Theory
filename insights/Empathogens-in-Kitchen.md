# 💖 Insight: 

## 🍽️ Observation 
It’s a bit wild to think about, but your spice rack might actually be a library of structural analogs. Many common foods contain molecular motifs that are remarkably similar to the functional groups found in the empathogens (both exogenous and endogenous) discussed in this repository.
### Examples: 
- **Fennel** contains **anethole**, which shares a similar phenylpropanoid structure to the common empathogens MDMA and MDA. It has shown anticancer properties due to its ability to induce apoptosis and impact critical signaling pathways  such as NF-κB, PI3K, Akt and mTOR [[1]](#ref1).
- **Nutmeg** contains **myrsticin** (methoxysafrole, 5-allyl-1-methoxy-2,3-methylenedioxybenzene) which shares a similar structure to methylenedioxyamphetamine (MDA), an exogenous empathogen [[2]](#ref2) This compound is also found in black pepper, dill, and parsley.
Sesame has sesamin which has methylenedioxy groups. In this repository, methylenedioxy groups are theorized to make the divergence of the phenethylamine electron gradient flattened out. This may allow for stronger and more coherent modulation of receptors (e.g., 5-HT and TAAR1). Although this molecule is too large to cross the blood-brain barrier, it may modulate positive epigenetic changes that lead to reduced systemic inflammation, reduced depression, and improved social cognition.
- **Parsley and celery (Apiole):** Apiole is a positional isomer of dillapiole, containing two methoxy groups and one methylenedioxy group on the aromatic ring.
- **Interesting Tangent: Berberine:** The isoquinilone system in berberine intercalates with DNA via $\pi$ - $\pi$ stacking. Ibogaine contains a fused isoquinuclidine-tetrahydroazepine ring system (a more rigid, comprehensive, cage-like structure of the isoquinilone motif in this thought experiment). is proposed that this DNA intercalation (e.g., via berberine or ibogaine) induces a stable attractor state or long-term potentiation of coherent states (child-like or embryogenic-genomic like).
- **Important Disclaimer:** These dietary interventions are not for everyone, and some of these can be toxic in high doses (e.g., myrsticin). Extreme caution should be exercised if on medications with some of these (e.g., berberine), as they can inhibit monoamine dioxidaise and cause medication to rise to dangerous levels in the blood. The discussion of iboga is strictly theoretical, and this repository does not advocate for its use, and it can be cardiotoxic.
## 🔬 Hypothesis & The Path Ahead
I’ve been personally experimenting with incorporating these specific foods into my daily diet for a few months (as someone on the autism spectrum). Subjectively, I’ve noticed a shift: an uptick in sociability and a deeper sense of empathy. This leads me to hypothesize that these molecules might exert a "micro-version" of the beneficial effects we see in more powerful tryptamines and empathogens.

### Research Questions
* Do these dietary compounds nudge our epigenetics in the same direction as traditional empathogens (e.g., upregulating $TAAR1$ or balancing $GTF2I$ for better social cognition), just at a much subtler scale?
* Can we "tune" our basic biology toward a more empathogenic state simply by being intentional with our grocery list?
* Since anethole is highly reactive, could it facilitate **Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS)** reactions with our own internal trace amines, making them "stick" to receptors like $TAAR1$ more effectively?

### Future Directions
* **Synergy & Immunity:** Measuring how these compounds perform when administered together. Does the sum of the parts create a stronger shield for the immune system or a more potent anticancer effect (e.g., stacking berberine with nutmeg and fennel)?
* **Mapping the Gradient:** Modeling how the divergence and curl of electron density in functional groups affects exactly how a molecule shakes hands with a receptor for substituted phenethylamines, including exploring how these naturally-occuring compounds might facilitate reactions that make endogenous versions of these phenethylamines.
* **The Metabolic Switch:** Exploring the hypothesis that the body can naturally convert myristicin into **MMDA**. If so, it suggests our diet could be directly upregulating endogenous empathogens, potentially mimicking the unique neurobiology seen in conditions like Williams syndrome.
## 💫 Future Directions
- Measuring the effects on immune system modulation and cancer biology of administration of these compounds in isolation and together. Concurrent administration of these compounds is thought to result in synergistic reactions and benefits.
- Modeling how the divergence and curl of the electron density of functional groups (e.g., methylenedioxy, phenethylamines) affects receptor binding.
- Exploring the hypothesis that myrsticin may convert into MMDA in biological systems, suggesting these common dietary interventions may lead to reactions in biological systems that upregulate endogenous empathogens (which may be naturally upregulated in Williams syndrome).
