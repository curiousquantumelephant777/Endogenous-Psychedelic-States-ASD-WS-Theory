# Endogenous-Empathogen-Theory
This research proposes a novel metabolic framework for understanding Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) and Williams Syndrome (WS) not as "deficits," but as distinct endogenous psychedelic states. I hypothesize that the phenotypic "mirroring" observed between these conditions (social withdrawal and sensory intensity in ASD versus hypersociability and social fearlessness in WS) is driven by divergent shunts in the methylation and trace amine pathways. Specifically, this model proposes that ASD is driven by an overactive INMT/DMT/bufotenin pathway (leading to continual sensory overload), whereas WS is characterized by an overactive PEA/TAAR1 pathway (possibly involving novel endogenous substituted phenethylamines), which drives the hypersociability. I propose that this INMT pathway, if overloaded in ASD, shunts away from the Kyurensin serotonergic pathway. Dysregulation in the Kyurensin pathway has been linked to immune system activation and potentially the accumulation of molecules that are toxic [1]. This could explain some of the neuroinflammation and methylation issues in ASD (e.g., microglia activation, MTHFR gene mutations seen in some cases of ASD). 
# William's Syndrome and Heart-Centered Orientation
The **"Heart-Centered" orientation** in Williams Syndrome may be characterized by TAAR1-mediated surges in Serotonin and Oxytocin. This represents a "Hyper-Integrated inner Torus/"heart-centered consciousness" state, where social trust is biologically unfettered. I hypothesize this is the phenotypic state of WS's social profile, which is also observed acutely after substituted (methylated) phenethylamine (e.g., MDMA) administration. My research aims to discover the endogenous empathogenic effects of William's Syndrome, which will pave the way for novel therapeutics to ameliorate the social deficits and sensory overload in ASD.
William's Syndrome is characterized by the deletion of several genes on the 7.11.23 locus, including GTFT2I, a gene known for its role in social connection. Conversely, a duplication of GTFT2I results in neuropsychiatric symptoms that mirror the social deficits seen in ASD. Interestingly, GTFT2I (and GTF2IRD1), is thought to have played a role in the domestication of wolves to dogs [2]. I'm particularly interested in mapping the genomic lineage of these traits. I propose the overactive INMT in people with ASD may be a conserved gene from the Incilius alvarius toad lineage (which produces bufotenin (5-HO-DMT) and 5-MeO-DMT, and the empathogenic phenotype of WS may be genetically linked to the domestication of wolves to dogs.
