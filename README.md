# Endogenous-Empathogen-Theory
This research proposes a novel metabolic framework for understanding Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) and Williams Syndrome (WS) not as "deficits," but as distinct endogenous psychedelic states. I hypothesize that the phenotypic "mirroring" observed between these conditions (social withdrawal and sensory intensity in ASD versus hypersociability and social fearlessness in WS) is driven by divergent shunts in the pineal and trace amine pathways. Specifically, this model proposes that ASD is driven by an overactive INMT/DMT/bufotenin pathway (leading to continual sensory overload), whereas WS is characterized by an overactive PEA/TAAR1 pathway (possibly involving novel endogenous substituted phenethylamines), which drives the hypersociability. I propose that this INMT pathway, if overloaded in ASD, shunts away from the Kyurensin serotonergic pathway. This could explain some of the neuroinflammation and methylation issues in ASD (e.g., microglia activation).
The **"Heart-Centered" orientation** in Wiliam's Syndrome may be characterized by TAAR1-mediated surges in Serotonin and Oxytocin. This represents a "Hyper-Integrated inner Torus/"heart-centered consciousness" state, where social trust is biologically unfettered. I hypothesize this is the phenotypic state of WS's social profile, which is also observed acutely after substituted (methylated) phenethylamine administration. My research aims to discover the endogenous empathogenic effects of William's Syndrome, which will pave the way for novel therapeutics to ameliorate the social deficits and sensory overload in ASD.
William's Syndrome is characterized by the deletion of several genes on the 7.11.23 locus, including GTFT2I, a gene known for its role in social connection.
